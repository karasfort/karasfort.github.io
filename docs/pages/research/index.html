Research

I am a social scientist interested in how and why people believe or like certain evidence or information. I use various methods including experiments, cross-sectional surveys, and panel surveys in my research. I am also trained in more advanced quantitative methods, such as SEM, social network analysis, and psychometrics. My work has received two top paper panel awards at the National Communication Association Conference (Social Cognition Division in 2025, Political Communication Division in 2025).

Dissertation

My dissertation aims to better understand how and why people find certain arguments or evidence persuasive. Specifically, I am interested in how variables such as similarity, expertise, and evidence/argument type interact with individual’s epistemic beliefs to influence perceived argument strength and persuasion. In this work, I draw on theories and concepts such as dual process models, processing fluency, narrative engagement and persuasion, and motivated reasoning.
